The shared evidence rule requires a number, a unit and a reason. In a scope these are product decisions that read like technical details. Pin each:

- Freshness: "running" means a status within 5 minutes; results cached 90 seconds, secrets 8 hours.
- Window, and what falls outside it: 7 days, with a full-history fallback so a device that has not reported recently still resolves.
- Settlement: a lag of days before a period is final, so late data is neither missed nor counted twice.
- Size limit, and what the person is told at it: a 5 GiB guard on one upload, with a message to split the file. A limit with no message is a silent failure.
- Retention: rotation at 2,000 MB per file; a 72-hour requirement proven by extrapolating a 20-minute sample, both numbers stated.
- Ceiling: a hard limit of 1,000 items per project, stated as a safe practical ceiling for this system.
- Clock: records dated in the future are ignored, against clock skew.
- Default page: raised from 50 to 200 on the owning side when a consumer that omitted the limit got cut-off lists. Never ask every consumer to pass a value.

- When a number is chosen by feel, the outcome says so and names what it was compared with. A guess that admits it gets corrected; one dressed as analysis gets defended.
- When the scope claims a gain in speed or cost, the outcome carries the number before, the target, and what the change does not solve.
- When the scope creates anything paid for, state the monthly price and its driver where the person reads it, for example a reviewer note giving an idle cost of about 110 US dollars a month while the services are parked. A new vendor or plan goes into the session's one batch; a cost inside an existing account goes there only above the two thresholds of the shared money line, and is otherwise decided and recorded with its number.
- When a schedule runs more often than anyone reads its result, running it less often is in scope.
- In a sandbox, scope the smallest and shortest of everything: the smallest instance type will do, and sandbox data is disposable. Where data is kept, scope the hardened settings, and never loosen them to let a teardown pass.
