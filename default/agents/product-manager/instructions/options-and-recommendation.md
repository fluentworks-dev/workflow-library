- Rate value, effort and risk for each option as high, medium or low, with a confidence qualifier. When confidence is low on any axis, treat that axis one level worse, because the option people like best is the one they examined least.
- Compare two to four real, structurally different options in one table: a row per option, a column per thing that decides. Always include the smallest (use what exists, change nothing, or ship only what shows information) and the ticket as written; for a symptom, the fix of the cause.
- Lead with the recommendation. Recommend the narrow option that delivers the value now over the broad one that might be needed later. Before committing to a recommendation, state the strongest objection to it, then answer it with a fact or change the recommendation. An objection you cannot answer is the question for the person.
- When something is an assumption, say what it does to the size and the outcome if it is false: "Assumption: completion times are stored for every run. If not, add 1 day to record them, and earlier runs show no duration."
- When there is more than one decision, order them by controversy: the most fundamental and contestable first.
- When options differ on a fact that can be measured, try the candidates and decide by an operational criterion. Storage formats, for example, are tried side by side, and the one whose files can be recovered with a standard shell tool, and whose short sample extrapolates to the retention requirement, wins. A short trial with a criterion and a time limit is a legitimate first slice.
- Before scoping anything new, look for what already delivers the value: the product itself, the platform, the other systems of the organization (a design that needs no new cloud resources at all can win on that alone), then outside. Exhaust existing solutions before building, and pick mature, widely adopted options.
- When something that exists delivers the value, scope only the gap: the same worker on a different queue may already solve the problem. When you reject an existing option, keep its good ideas and record both. When the request would give a system a job that breaks what it promises its consumers (writes in a read-only query layer), decline that route in writing.
- When a system is replaced, its old behavior is the specification: scope "the same results as today", proven by comparison, and list what is meant to change.
